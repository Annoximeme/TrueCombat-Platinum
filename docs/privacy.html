Privacy

What we count, and what we never will

We want to know which pages are worth reading and where new players hear about the game. We do not need to know who you are to find that out, so we never ask.

01

The short version

  • No tracking cookies, no ad trackers, no analytics companies. Nothing from Google, Meta or anyone else is loaded on this site.
  • We count visits, not visitors. The website keeps daily totals and nothing that points back to a person.
  • Your browser says no, we listen. With Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track switched on, you are not counted at all.
  • The launcher shares crash and hardware data only if you say yes. It asks once, with two equal buttons, and you can change your answer at any time.
  • A real person answers. Write to gianni@truecombat.eu for a copy of your data, a correction or a deletion. We reply within one month.
02

This website

When a page opens, your browser sends one short message to our own server: the page, the site that linked you here (the site name only, never the full address), the campaign tag in the link if there is one, the page language, and whether the screen is a phone, tablet or desktop size. A click on a download button sends one more message saying a download started.

To count you once per day instead of on every page, the server makes a one-way fingerprint from your connection address and browser, mixed with a random secret that only exists for that day. The same fingerprint lets a download count toward the site that brought you here that day. At midnight UTC the secret and every fingerprint are deleted. After that, nobody can connect one day to the next, including us.

The server looks up the country of your connection address in a list it keeps on the box itself, keeps the two-letter country code and forgets the address. No outside service is asked.

What we keep

Daily totals per page, linking site, campaign, country, screen size and language, and how many of those visits led to a download. Devlog posts also keep how many people read and finished them, and their star ratings. These totals are kept for as long as the site exists.

What we never keep

  • Connection (IP) addresses, browser details or device information
  • Cookies, or anything else stored in your browser for counting
  • Full addresses of the pages that sent you here
  • Anything that links one visit, rating or download to a person

Your browser keeps a few small notes for the site itself, never for counting: your devlog star ratings, which devlog posts you have already seen, a short-lived copy of the release notes and, while you link a PC to your account, the one-time link code. Clearing your browser data removes them.

Live data such as servers and leaderboards comes from api.truecombat.eu, and release notes are copied onto this site, so your browser never asks GitHub for them. The site is delivered through Cloudflare, which keeps it fast and shields it from attacks. The trailer comes from YouTube only after you click play, and downloads come from GitHub. Like any web server, each of these sees your connection address while it answers.

03

The game and the launcher

  • Online play. Game servers see your connection address, as every online game does. Our moderation tools keep it for up to 90 days to deal with cheating and abuse, and a ban record keeps the address that was banned.
  • Leaderboards. Ranked matches on official and verified servers record your in-game name, a player ID made from your game key, and your match statistics. That is what fills the leaderboards and your public profile, and the records are kept for as long as the leaderboards exist, because every rank is built from them. Each match also keeps a one-way code of your connection address, so two players on one connection never count against each other; after 90 days that code is removed. Account holders can hide their profile at any time. Without an account, write to us and we take your name off the public pages.
  • Anti-cheat. Official servers use Easy Anti-Cheat, which runs under the Epic Games privacy policy.
  • Crash reports and memory dumps stay on your PC. They only reach us if you attach them to a bug report yourself. If you choose to share launcher data, only the crash location is sent, as described below.
  • Bug reports you send from the game include what you write, a screenshot and technical details such as the map, frame rate and graphics card, so we can reproduce the problem. A report and its screenshot are deleted 90 days after the issue is closed.
  • Chat and voice. Text and voice chat travel through the game server to the other players. Our servers do not record voice. Text chat on official servers is kept for 30 days so that reports of abuse can be checked.
04

Crash and hardware data from the launcher

From launcher 2.6.0, the Play page asks once whether you want to share anonymous crash and hardware data. Nothing is sent until you choose Share, and nothing at all if you choose No thanks. The same switch is in Settings > Privacy, and turning it off stops sharing straight away.

When sharing is on, the launcher sends at most one short report a day to api.truecombat.eu, and only when there is something new to report:

  • Crashes. For each new crash: whether the game or the server crashed, the Windows error code, the place in the program where it happened (a file name and a position in it, such as renderer_dx12_x64.dll+0x1a2b3), up to five steps leading to it in the same form, and how many minutes the game had been running. Crash reports and memory dumps themselves stay on your PC.
  • Game starts. How many times the game was started since the last report, how many of those were Safe starts, and the game and launcher version.
  • Hardware, at most once a month. Your Windows version, processor model and thread count, memory and video memory in whole gigabytes, graphics card model, whether DirectX 12 works, desktop resolution and refresh rate, and the launcher language.

A report never contains your player name, game key, player ID, an install ID, file or folder names on your PC, server addresses or your connection address. Nothing in one report links it to the next, so we cannot tell whether two reports came from the same PC. As with the website, our server looks up the country of the connection, keeps only the two-letter code and forgets the address.

We keep crash locations and daily counts of starts and crashes per game version for 90 days after we last saw them, and monthly counts per hardware value (for example, how many launchers reported a given graphics card) for 13 months. Memory is grouped into ranges so an unusual amount cannot single anyone out. Because no report carries anything that points to you, there is nothing we could look up to show or delete "your" data, and that is by design.

See it for yourself. Settings > Privacy > What is shared in the launcher shows the exact report your PC would send next, before it leaves your computer.

05

Player accounts

Accounts are optional. Nobody needs one to play. If you make one, you sign in with Discord, and we ask Discord for your user ID and name only. The Discord sign-in token is thrown away straight after.

  • What we keep. Your Discord user ID and name, the player name you pick, the PCs you linked (a code made from each PC's game key, plus the label you gave it), any passkeys you add (public keys only), your profile settings, and your previous name for 30 days after a rename.
  • Staying signed in. A sign-in cookie on truecombat.eu keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. The account needs it to work, and it is used for nothing else. Each sign-in is logged with its connection address for 30 days, so we can spot a stolen account.
  • Your profile. Your profile is public unless you set it to hidden, and your Discord name only shows if you switch it on. Accounts are for players aged 13 and over.
  • Devlog comments. A comment you post shows your player name, what you wrote and when, to everyone who reads the devlog. You can edit it for 15 minutes and delete it at any time. Your first comment may wait for a moderator before it shows. We keep which comments you liked or reported, so a like counts once and a report reaches the moderators. Handled reports are deleted after 90 days, as are comments a moderator removed. A comment is never stored with your connection address.

We hold this because you asked for an account (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)), and we keep it until you delete it or stop using it. If you neither play nor sign in for a year, your reserved name is released. After two years without either, we delete the account, with a message on Discord a month before. Your account page has a button that downloads everything we hold about your account, and one that deletes it for good: the account, linked PCs, names, devlog comments, sessions and sign-in log. Match statistics stay on the leaderboards, no longer tied to your account. If the account was banned, the ban keeps a one-way code of your Discord ID so it cannot be dodged with a new account.

06

Moderation, Discord and support

  • Reports and appeals. When a player is reported or banned, moderators keep what they need to decide: the report, the player name and IDs, and evidence such as screenshots or match recordings. Evidence is deleted after a year unless its case is still open. A ban, and any appeal against it with the contact details you choose to give, is kept with the ban record.
  • Discord. Our Discord server and bot see what Discord shares with every server you join. If you link your account, the bot can look up your player name and statistics to post match results and hand out roles. Everything else on Discord falls under Discord's privacy policy.
  • Support on Ko-fi. Donations go through Ko-fi. We store the amount and the name you give, and show that name only if you made your support public. We never store your email address, payment details or message.
07

Who else handles data

We never sell data or share it for advertising. These companies handle data on our behalf, or run a service you use with us:

  • Hostinger, our server host: game servers, website, statistics and accounts.
  • Cloudflare delivers and protects the website.
  • Zoho handles our email, in its EU data centres.
  • GitHub serves the downloads and the source code, and holds our private nightly server backups. Account data inside those backups is encrypted.
  • Discord for account sign-in and our community. Epic Games for Easy Anti-Cheat on official servers. Ko-fi for donations. YouTube for the trailer, only after you click play.

Some of these companies are outside the EU. Transfers rely on an EU adequacy decision, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or the decision for the United Kingdom, or on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

08

Your choices and rights

To stop being counted on this website, switch on Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track in your browser. Blocking api.truecombat.eu/insights works too, and the site keeps working exactly the same.

Your rights. You can ask for a copy of your data, have it corrected or deleted, restrict how we use it, or receive it in a portable form. You can also object to anything we do on the basis of legitimate interest. Account holders can download or delete everything themselves on the account page. For anything else, write to gianni@truecombat.eu. We reply within one month, and may ask you to show the data is yours, for example by writing from the account in question. All of the counting code is in the public repository, so you never have to take our word for it.

Who is responsible. TrueCombat: Platinum is made by Team Terminus. The data controller is Gianni Goossens (RaziC*), based in Belgium and reachable at gianni@truecombat.eu. Website counting, protecting the game servers, moderation, leaderboards and backups rest on our legitimate interest in running a fair, secure game that works (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), with the safeguards described here. Accounts rest on your request for one (Article 6(1)(b)). Launcher data and bug reports are only sent with your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time. If you believe we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority; in Belgium, that is the Data Protection Authority.
