Privacy

What we count, and what we never will

We want to know which pages are worth reading and where new players hear about the game. We do not need to know who you are to find that out, so we never ask.

The short versionThis websiteThe gameYour choicesChanges
01

The short version

  • No tracking cookies, no ad trackers, no analytics companies. Nothing from Google, Meta or anyone else is loaded on this site.
  • We count visits, not visitors. The website keeps daily totals and nothing that points back to a person.
  • Your browser says no, we listen. With Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track switched on, you are not counted at all.
  • The game sends usage data only if you ask it to. That switch is off until you turn it on.
02

This website

When a page opens, your browser sends one short message to our own server: the page, the site that linked you here (the site name only, never the full address), the campaign tag in the link if there is one, the page language, and whether the screen is a phone, tablet or desktop size. A click on a download button sends one more message saying a download started.

To count you once per day instead of on every page, the server makes a one-way fingerprint from your connection address and browser, mixed with a random secret that only exists for that day. The same fingerprint lets a download count toward the site that brought you here that day. At midnight UTC the secret and every fingerprint are deleted. After that, nobody can connect one day to the next, including us.

The server looks up the country of your connection address in a list it keeps on the box itself, keeps the two-letter country code and forgets the address. No outside service is asked.

What we keep

Daily totals per page, linking site, campaign, country, screen size and language, and how many of those visits led to a download. Devlog posts also keep how many people read and finished them, and their star ratings. These totals are kept for as long as the site exists.

What we never keep

  • Connection (IP) addresses, browser details or device information
  • Cookies, or anything else stored in your browser for counting
  • Full addresses of the pages that sent you here
  • Anything that links one visit, rating or download to a person

Your devlog star ratings are remembered in your own browser, so the stars stay filled in when you come back. That note never leaves your computer and is not used for counting.

Some pages fetch live data from api.truecombat.eu (servers, leaderboards) and patch notes from the GitHub API. The trailer comes from YouTube only after you click play. As with any website, these servers see your connection address while they answer.

03

The game and the launcher

  • Online play. Game servers see your connection address, as every online game does. Our moderation tools keep it for up to 90 days to deal with cheating and abuse, and a ban record keeps the address that was banned.
  • Leaderboards. Ranked matches on official and verified servers record your in-game name, a player ID made from your game key, and your match statistics. That is what fills the leaderboards and your public profile.
  • Anti-cheat. Official servers use Easy Anti-Cheat, which runs under the Epic Games privacy policy.
  • Crash reports stay on your PC. They only reach us if you attach them to a bug report yourself.
  • Bug reports you send from the game include what you write, a screenshot and technical details such as the map, frame rate and graphics card, so we can reproduce the problem.

Coming soon: optional usage data. A future update adds a switch in the launcher that shares anonymous performance and crash data to help us fix problems on the hardware you actually play on. It is off unless you switch it on, and this page will list exactly what it sends before it ships.

04

Your choices

To stop being counted on this website, switch on Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track in your browser. Blocking api.truecombat.eu/insights works too, and the site keeps working exactly the same.

Questions, or want something removed? Ask us on Discord or open an issue on GitHub. All of the counting code is in the public repository, so you never have to take our word for it.

We count website visits because we have a legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), with the safeguards described above. Usage data from the game is only collected with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in the launcher. TrueCombat: Platinum is run by Team Terminus, led by RaziC*. If you believe we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority; in Belgium, where we are based, that is the Data Protection Authority.

05
